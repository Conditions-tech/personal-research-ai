<jats:p>The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (<jats:italic>TESS</jats:italic>) discovered several new planet candidates that need to be confirmed and characterized with ground-based observations. This is the case of Ross 176, a late K-type star that hosts a promising water-world candidate planet. The star has a radius of <jats:italic>R</jats:italic><jats:sub>⋆</jats:sub> = 0.569 ± 0.020 R<jats:sub>⊙</jats:sub> and a mass of <jats:italic>M</jats:italic><jats:sub>⋆</jats:sub> = 0.577 ± 0.024 M<jats:sub>⊙</jats:sub>. We constrained the planetary mass using spectroscopic data from CARMENES, an instrument that has already played a major role in confirming the planetary nature of the transit signal detected by <jats:italic>TESS</jats:italic>. We used Gaussian Processes (GP) to improve the analysis because the host star has a relatively strong activity that affects the radial velocity dataset. In addition, we applied a GP to the <jats:italic>TESS</jats:italic> light curves to reduce the correlated noise in the detrended dataset. The stellar activity indicators show a strong signal that is related to the stellar rotation period of ∼32 days. This stellar activity signal was also confirmed on the <jats:italic>TESS</jats:italic> light curves. Ross 176 b is an inner hot transiting planet with a low-eccentricity orbit of e = 0.25 ± 0.04, an orbital period of P ~ 5 days, and an equilibrium temperature of <jats:italic>T</jats:italic><jats:sub>eq</jats:sub> ~ 682 K. With a radius of <jats:italic>R</jats:italic><jats:sub>p</jats:sub> = 1.84 ± 0.08 R<jats:sub>⊕</jats:sub> (4% precision), a mass of <jats:italic>M</jats:italic><jats:sub>p</jats:sub> = 4.57<jats:sub>−0.93</jats:sub><jats:sup>+0.89</jats:sup>M<jats:sub>⊕</jats:sub> (20% precision), and a mean density of ρ<jats:sub>p</jats:sub> = 4.03<jats:sub>−0.81</jats:sub><jats:sup>+0.49</jats:sup>g cm<jats:sup>−3</jats:sup>, the composition of Ross 176 b might be consistent with a water-world scenario. Moreover, Ross 176 b is a promising target for atmospheric characterization, which might lead to more information on the existence, formation and composition of water worlds. This detection increases the sample of planets orbiting K-type stars. This sample is valuable for investigating the valley of planets with small radii around this type of star. This study also shows that the dual detection of space- and ground-based telescopes is efficient for confirm new planets.</jats:p>
